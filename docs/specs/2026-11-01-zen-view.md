Status: design, not built. Revision 1 (2026-11-01). ## Intent

The GUI has two views today: Standard (the Tokyo Night map with its motion) and Geek (black, monospace, still).
Both show the whole net at once: every base, the HUD alert with its count, the dock of dialogs, the notices.
The boss wants a third view, **Zen**, for the hours when he is not steering the net but answering it:

- 「先處理待辦，沒事時陪著一個 session」: when something waits for him, the whole page shows only that one item; after he answers, cancels, or skips it, the next one; when nothing waits, the page shows the session he last talked to, its live log and its composer. Esc, or a small control in a corner, returns to the Standard view.
- 「不用那麼嚴格，只要不要顯示待辦數量或是還有東西需要處理」: the page never shows how many items wait, nor any hint that more are queued: no counts, no dots, no "also queued". Other numbers may stay where they help.
- 「呼吸圈＋三秒停頓，墨色」: a breathing circle (inhale 4 s, exhale 6 s) when nothing waits, a very slow drift of the background colour, and after an item is handled a 3 s breathing pause before the next one (a tap skips it). The palette is "ink": a warm black ground, rice-paper off-white text, few accents. Dark only.
- Sound: background music from tracks the boss generates with Suno (a separate worker produces them into `gui/sounds/zen/`), looped with a crossfade, off by default, on and off with one tap, with a choice of track; effects synthesized with Web Audio: a soft singing-bowl tone when a new item takes the screen, a wooden-fish (木魚) knock when one is done, a lower bowl on an error. The volume follows the settings page's Volume. The sounds of Standard and Geek stay as they are.
- Zen is a view like the other two: `/boss gui view zen` sets the server's default, and each device can choose it on the settings page.

## Scope

- In: the view's place beside Standard and Geek (the device's choice, the server's default, `gui.json`); which waiting things become Zen items, their order, and what each item shows and how it is answered; the companion session and its fallbacks; the pause, the skip, and leaving and coming back; motion, colour, and the calm rules; the effects and the music, with the phones' autoplay rules; the asset budget; reminders and the tab icon in Zen; both languages; the demo states, screenshots, and the GIF; the build steps, tests, and docs.
- Out: any change to Standard or Geek beyond the fourth button of the view control, the header's way back into Zen, and moving `intel` out of `layer.ts` (**Design**); any new server route or any change to what a paired device may do; a light variant; a Zen in the terminal (the tower dashboard, the sidebar); producing the music (the Suno worker's task; this spec states what it must deliver, **Music tracks**); the desk shell's own colour for `zen` (straw-boss-desk; **Docs to change** names the follow-up).

## Terms

- **Item**: one thing that waits for the boss, as Zen shows it: a unit that waits on an answer (mark `waiting`, or a dialog pending) or a project trust question. Exactly the alerts of rank 0 (`rankOf`, `gui/alerts.ts:26`), the set the HUD alert button serves in Standard.
- **Current item**: the one item on the screen. There is at most one.
- **Resting item**: an item the boss skipped; it cannot become current until its rest ends (**Skip and rest**).
- **Companion**: the session Zen shows while no item is current (**The companion**).
- **Pause**: the 3 s breath between an item leaving the screen and what comes next.
- **Away**: this page left Zen for Standard (Esc or the corner control) without changing the device's choice (**Leaving and coming back**).
- **Effects**: the three synthesized sounds (bowl, knock, low bowl). **Music**: the looped Suno tracks.

## Design in one page

```text
<html data-view="zen">                       one layer over the whole page; Standard's DOM is display:none under it
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ [Standard]                                        [♪] [⌄]    │  corner controls only, 44 px, quiet until hovered or focused
│                                                              │
│                ( item on the screen )                        │  an item: who asks, the question, its answer card,
│        ops-main · Harbor API                                 │           Send / Cancel / Not now — nothing about others
│        "Robot Hand — Deploy needs your sudo password"        │
│        ( ) Type it into a new pane   ( ) Put it in my prompt │
│        [Send answer]  [Cancel]  [Not now]                    │
│                                                              │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
answered → knock → outcome 1.2 s → pause 3 s (one breath; a tap skips) → next item, or:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ [Standard]                                        [♪] [⌄]    │
│                         ◯  (breathes 4 s in, 6 s out)        │  the circle behind the log, faint
│   tower · Harbor API                                         │  the companion: the session he last talked to
│   … newest entries of its log, live …                        │
│   [ composer                                         Send ]  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Zen is a value of the existing view: `zen` beside `standard` and `geek`, for the device and for the server. Nothing on the server changes but the accepted value.
- The Zen layer is a chunk of its own (`gui/zen.ts` with `gui/zen.css`), loaded only on a page whose view is Zen. It reads the same snapshots, alerts, and dialogs as Standard and answers through the same cards and routes; it adds no server route.
- A pure model (`gui/zenmodel.ts`) decides what is on the screen: the current item, resting items, the pause, the companion. The DOM follows it, and the sounds follow its transitions, so the rules are tested without a browser, as `overlay.ts` is.
- Standard keeps running underneath as it does for a hidden page: it holds the newest snapshot and draws it when the boss leaves Zen, so leaving shows the net as it is.

## The view

### Choice, default, and record

- `Choice` becomes `"auto" | "standard" | "geek" | "zen"` and `View` `"standard" | "geek" | "zen"` (`gui/view.ts`); `choiceOf` accepts `zen`; `THEME` gets `zen`, the ink ground (**Ink**), so the browser's bar and an installed app's bar match.
- Auto stays as it is: it follows the server's default when one is set, else Standard, or Geek when the screen asks for more contrast. Auto never picks Zen by itself; only the server's default or the device's choice does. A server default of `zen` wins over `prefers-contrast: more`, as `geek` and `standard` do today; Zen's text meets 7 : 1 (**Ink**), so a device that asks for contrast is still served.
- The settings page's view control gets a fourth button, **Zen** (`data-choice="zen"`), and the Auto button reads **Follow server (Zen)** when the server's default is `zen`. The four buttons wrap on a phone the way the three do with `data-following`.
- `VIEWS` in `src/server/guiprefs.ts` gets `zen`; `gui-view` accepts it, and its refusal reads "The default view is auto, standard, geek, or zen"; `GuiRecord.view` may be `"zen"`; `src/host.ts` names it in its words (`Zen`); `BOSS_ARGUMENTS` gets `gui view zen`: "Default view: Zen (one waiting item at a time, then a session for company) for devices that follow the server".
- `/boss gui view zen` therefore needs no new path: the control socket stores it, every page gets it in its meta, hello, and `view` event, and a page that follows the server switches at once. A page of an older build that hears `zen` reads it as Auto (`choiceOf`), and a stream from another build reloads the page anyway (`rebuilt`, `gui/main.ts`).

### What is hidden, and the first frame

- Under `[data-view="zen"]` the main stylesheet hides every part of Standard's page (`display: none` on the stage, header, HUD, dock, tiles, timeline, banner, drawer) and shows `#zen`, an empty `<section id="zen" hidden>` in `index.html` that the chunk fills (named by `aria-label`; the page's `<main>` stays the map's). The first-frame block (ground, circle, hiding) is in `style.css`, about 1 KB, so a page that opens in Zen paints ink from its first frame with no flash of Standard; everything else is in `gui/zen.css`, loaded with the chunk.
- The connection overlay (`gui/connect.ts`) still covers the page while there is no live state, in Zen as in the other views; `zen.css` gives it the ink colours.
- In Zen, the hotkeys of the dialogs (Space, F) do nothing, the phone drawer and its swipe do not act, and the tab icon shows no count (`badge` draws the plain icon while Zen is shown).

### Leaving and coming back

- **Esc** leaves Zen for Standard, unless something else took it: a modal (`MODAL`), the composer keeping it (a box with text, an open question), or any handler that called `preventDefault`. A text field of an answer card with text in it takes the first Esc as a blur; the next one leaves.
- The **corner control** (top left, reading **Standard**) does the same on any screen, a phone's included.
- Leaving is **away** for this page only: `data-view` becomes `standard`, `<html>` gets `data-zen-away`, the device's choice stays `zen`, and nothing is stored. Standard shows the newest snapshot (the hidden-page path of `show`), and:
  - with an item on the screen, the page opens that item's dialog on top of the dock (`stack.show(alert)`), so the boss goes on with it in Standard;
  - with the companion on the screen, the page goes to the companion's session route;
  - during a pause, the route the page is on.
- While away, Standard's header shows a **Zen** button (beside the gear) that returns to Zen; it is hidden whenever the device's resolved view is not Zen. A reload, or a new page of this device, opens in Zen again.
- The settings route is never covered: a page that opens on `settings` (a link, the QR flows) shows the settings page, and choosing Zen there leaves the page on settings, away; leaving settings (Back, Escape, the gear) enters Zen.

## Items

### Which kinds

- An item is an alert of rank 0 from `alertsOf`: a unit (tower, main, member, subagent, outside unit) whose mark is `waiting` or that has a dialog pending, and a project trust question. It is the same set Standard's HUD alert serves, so the two views never disagree about what waits.
- Not items: a unit that failed, a monitor that failed or went quiet, a unit that finished (rank 1). Standard keeps them in its Notices; Zen shows none of them (O1). The tower memory's open waits and proposals are not alerts today and are not items (they show in the tower's comms, which a companion that is a tower shows in its log).
- An opening this page runs (`ownOpenings`) is not an alert and so not an item.

### Order

- The next item is the eligible one (not resting) that has waited longest: by the unit's `since` (the time it took its present state, the same on every device), or for a trust question the earliest `at` of its agents; an item without either takes the time this page first saw it. Ties go by key.
- A current item stays until it leaves (**When an item leaves**): an older item that appears meanwhile (a page that just connected, a unit that started waiting while the stream was down) never replaces it.
- An item that arrives while another is current, or during a pause, makes no sound, no motion, and no change on the screen. It is considered when the screen is next free.

### What an item shows

One card in the middle of the page, at most 40 rem wide, the whole width on a phone:

- **Who**: the unit's name, its role, and its base's label (`deps.label`); for a trust question its title and where the agents run (`whereOf`). No unit plate with its state glow: the card is the state.
- **What it said**: the unit's last line (`intel(items).said`), fetched once when the item takes the screen with `history ?limit=12`, as the dialog does. `intel` moves to `gui/intel.ts` (with `Intel`), imported by `layer.ts` and `zen.ts`, so the Zen chunk does not pull in the overlay layer; `layer.ts` keeps its behaviour.
- **The answer**, by kind:
  - a dialog that can be answered: `mountAsk` on the card's own root, with Send, the Cancel of `cancellable` (owner takes a cancel; on a paired device only where some choice is the device's to give), the per-choice `hereOnly` rule, Copy command, the outcome line, and Check again, exactly as in Standard;
  - a dialog shown read-only (two at once, one the server cannot match, any `secret_drop`): the same read-only panel, whose "answer it in the terminal" line stays; its Show terminal becomes **Open in Standard**, which leaves Zen (away) on the unit's session in terminal mode (`focus.terminal(key)`); a `secret_drop` has no such button on a paired device, as today;
  - a trust question: `trustPanel`, as in the dialog;
  - a unit that waits with no dialog (it asked in words): its last line, large, and a composer to that unit (`mountComposer`), so the boss replies in words.
- **Not now**: always shown, on every item, whatever else waits (**Skip and rest**). It never says or implies whether another item exists.
- Nothing on the card or anywhere in Zen counts or lists items: no "also queued", no tally, no faces of other units, no dot, no badge.
- When the item takes the screen, the focus goes to the card's first answer control (`ask.enter()`, else the composer's box, else the card) if the page has the focus and the boss is not typing elsewhere. A polite live region says "<name> asks" (or the trust title) once; it never says a number.

### Answering

The card posts through the same routes as Standard: `sessions/<key>/answer` for a dialog (`bodyOf`, `cancelOf`), `trust/answer` for a trust question, `sessions/<key>/input` for the composer. Nothing about who may answer changes:

- this computer's page and a paired device (Tailscale or Wi-Fi) see the same items; the device rules are those of `mountAsk` (`remote` from the host name), `trustPanel`, and the server (`control.ts`), unchanged: a `hereOnly` choice is disabled on a paired device, a `secret_drop` is read-only everywhere, the LAN overlay mark and `BOSS_LOCAL` refuse what they refuse today;
- an answer is never sent twice: Check again resends the same `answerId`, as in Standard.

### When an item leaves

An item leaves the screen in one of five ways; each is one transition of the model, with one sound at most:

| How | Sound | Then |
|---|---|---|
| The boss's answer or cancel is accepted | the knock, at the accepted outcome | the outcome line shows for 1.2 s (`accepted`), then the pause |
| **Not now** | none | the item rests; the pause |
| The item stops waiting by itself (answered in the terminal or on another device, the dialog closed, the unit moved on, a trust question settled) | the knock, with the line "Answered elsewhere" for 1.2 s | the pause |
| The unit leaves the net, or its pane closes | none | the pause |
| The unit fails while on the screen (mark `error`) | the low bowl, with its error line for 1.2 s | the pause |

- An answer that is refused, stale, busy, or not confirmed (`said` gives the error tone) plays the low bowl and leaves the card on the screen with its outcome, as Standard's dialog does; a stale answer's item then leaves by itself on the next snapshot, and that exit plays nothing (one sound per thing the boss did).
- A new dialog on the same unit while it is current (another digest) is the same item with a new card (`mountAsk` starts afresh for another dialog); it plays nothing.

### Skip and rest

- **Not now** rests the item for 10 minutes (O2). While it rests it is not eligible; when the rest ends it is eligible again in its place by age.
- A rest ends early when the item's question changes: a new dialog (another `dialog` and `digest`), or a unit that stopped waiting and waits again.
- Rests are this page's, in memory: a reload forgets them. Standard is not affected: its queue still holds the item.

### The pause

- After an item leaves, the card fades out and the breathing circle takes the middle of the screen for 3 s: one slow inhale over the 3 s (scale from 0.72 to 1). Then the next eligible item takes the screen with the bowl, or, with none, the companion fades in, silently.
- A tap or click anywhere, Enter, or Space ends the pause at once. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the circle stands still and the pause is still 3 s.
- While the page is hidden, a pause that would end ends at once when the page is shown again (no one was there to breathe).

## The companion

### Which session

- **Last talked to** is the session this device last sent a message to from a composer, in any view: the Standard session view's composer, the companion's, or an item's reply composer. A dialog answer does not count: answering is not talking (O4).
- It is kept in `localStorage` `sb.zen.with` as `{ key, ws, at }`, written after the server accepted the send (`input` answered 2xx). `gui/zenwith.ts` (eager, about 30 lines) wraps the `Post` given to `mountComposer` in `session.ts` and in Zen; `composer.ts` does not change.
- **Fallbacks**, first that holds, against the newest snapshot:
  1. the stored unit, while the net lists it with `control` and not `ended`;
  2. the tower of the stored unit's base, when that base still has a tower;
  3. the tower of the head base (`Base.head`);
  4. none: the breathing circle alone in the middle, the line "Nothing waits for you." and the corner controls. ("Nothing waits" is not a count.)
- The companion is chosen when it is about to show and kept while it shows; a unit that leaves the net while it is the companion is replaced by the next fallback at the next snapshot.
- An ally run or an event run (a route with no unit) is never a companion: a send to one records its tower.

### What it shows

- The session's name and base label in a quiet header; its log (`mountChat`, the same chunk and code as the session view) opened at the newest entry, with the oldest visible entries fading under the top edge; its live line (`liveNote`); and its composer at the bottom, above a phone's keyboard as in the session view. The boss may scroll back; new entries follow as in the session view.
- No bars (queue, todos, monitors), no dossier, no terminal mode, no Quit, no tower notices: those are Standard's. The composer's own notes and queued-message words stay (they are numbers that help, not items).
- The breathing circle stands behind the log, centred, at low opacity, and the background drifts (**Motion**).
- The stream watches the companion's key while it shows (`watchNow` in `main.ts` replaces `watchOf(current)` as the stream's watch, restarting the stream when it changes); history events go to Zen as they go to the focus views.
- While the boss types in the companion's composer (the box has the focus and text), an item that becomes eligible waits; it takes the screen when he sends, empties the box, or moves the focus out of it (O10). The draft stays in the composer's own store.

## Motion, colour, and calm

### Ink

The palette, dark only (`prefers-color-scheme` is not read):

| Token | Value | Use | Contrast on the ground |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--zen-ground` | `#14110e` | the page | — |
| `--zen-ground-2` | `#1b1612` | the drift's other ground | — |
| `--zen-paper` | `#ebe3d3` | text | ≥ 7 : 1 on both grounds |
| `--zen-ash` | `#a69d8e` | secondary text, the log's older lines | ≥ 7 : 1 on both grounds |
| `--zen-line` | `#6e6458` | borders, the circle's ring | ≥ 3 : 1 |
| `--zen-seal` | `#c4553a` | the focus ring and an error's mark (vermilion seal); never body text | ≥ 3 : 1 |
| `--zen-moss` | `#8fa58a` | an accepted outcome's mark | ≥ 3 : 1 |

- The values are the starting point; the test (**Tests**) computes every text colour at 7 : 1 or more on both grounds and every border and the focus ring at 3 : 1 or more, from `zen.css` and the first-frame block, as `geek-contrast.test.ts` does for Geek. The visual check may move a value within those bounds.
- Type: a serif stack for the card and the companion's header (`ui-serif, "Iowan Old Style", "Songti TC", "Noto Serif TC", PMingLiU, serif`), the page's sans for the log and the composer, monospace where the log already uses it. No font is shipped (the built page keeps its one font).
- An outcome, a state, or an error is always said in words; colour only underlines it.

### Motion

- **Breathing circle**: a ring and a faint radial fill, `min(48vmin, 320px)` across, one CSS animation of `transform` and `opacity` only: 10 s per breath, 4 s in (scale 0.72 to 1, ease-in-out) and 6 s out. It runs while nothing is current (the companion, or none) and drives the pause.
- **Drift**: two full-page layers (`--zen-ground` and `--zen-ground-2` in a soft radial gradient) cross-fade by `opacity` over 90 s, back and forth. Only compositor properties move, so the page does not repaint per frame.
- Cards fade in and out (opacity, 400 ms); nothing slides or bounces.
- **Calm rules**: Zen is not Geek, so `calm.ts` runs its timer in Zen: after 30 s without input `data-calm` stills the circle (at its exhaled size) and the drift (on `--zen-ground`), and any input brings them back. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing moves at all, from the first frame (the stylesheet's media query; `calm.ts` already does nothing there). Under `forced-colors` the system colours take over: the drift layers are hidden, the circle is a `CanvasText` outline, and the card a plain border.
- **Phone and desktop**: one layout that scales: the card centred with the corner controls in the safe-area insets; on a phone the card takes the width, long questions scroll inside it with the existing more-below cue (`cue`, `gui/ask.ts`); every control is at least 44 by 44 px; no sideways scroll at 320 px.

## Sound

### Effects

Synthesized in `gui/zensound.ts` with Web Audio, no files:

| Effect | When | Recipe (starting point, tuned in the live check) |
|---|---|---|
| **Bowl** | an item takes the screen | partials at 228 Hz × 1, 2.71, 5.15, 8.42 with gains 1, 0.45, 0.2, 0.08; each partial two sine oscillators 1.2 Hz apart (the bowl's beating); 15 ms attack, exponential decay with time constants 2.4, 1.4, 0.8, 0.4 s; 5 s long; peak 0.35 of the effects bus |
| **Knock** (木魚) | an item is done (**When an item leaves**) | a sine from 560 Hz falling to 480 Hz over 40 ms plus a 25 ms noise burst through a band-pass at 1.1 kHz (Q 6); decay time constant 70 ms; 250 ms long; peak 0.5 |
| **Low bowl** | an error of an item on the screen or of the boss's answer | the bowl's partials on 146 Hz, decay time constants two thirds of the bowl's, a 1 % downward bend over its length; peak 0.3 |

- Each recipe is a plain object (partials, envelopes, filter) that a pure function turns into scheduled nodes on any `BaseAudioContext`, so tests check the schedule with a fake context.
- **On and off**: the effects follow the settings page's Sound switch (`sb.sound`, off by default), the one switch that lets the page make sounds (O3). Zen never plays `notify.ts`'s clips, and Standard and Geek never play Zen's effects.
- **Volume**: the settings' Volume (`sb.volume`), as the square of it, read at each play, so a change on the settings page applies at once.
- While the music plays, an effect ducks it by 6 dB over 0.3 s and lets it back over 1.5 s.

### Music

- `gui/zenmusic.ts`, a chunk of its own loaded only when the music is turned on (or is on at load and the first gesture comes). It lists the tracks from `gui/sounds/zen/tracks.ts` (file, English and Chinese titles) and gets their URLs with `import.meta.glob("./sounds/zen/*.mp3", { query: "?url", import: "default", eager: true })`, which embeds only the URLs, never the bytes.
- **Playback**: two `<audio preload="none">` elements, each through `createMediaElementSource` and a gain of its own into the music bus (an iPhone ignores an element's `volume`, and honours a gain). The chosen track plays on one; 6 s before its end the other starts the same track from 0 and the two cross-fade (equal-power curves over 6 s), so the loop has no gap (an MP3's encoder padding makes `loop = true` click). Nothing is decoded into an `AudioBuffer` (a three-minute stereo track would take about 60 MB of memory).
- **Level**: the music bus is the effects' level × 0.5, under the same Volume.
- **Controls**: the corner's **♪** toggles the music with one tap (`aria-pressed`, the state in a word in its label: "Music: on" / "Music: off"); the **⌄** beside it opens a native popover (as `menu.ts`'s) with the tracks as radio buttons and **Try** for the three effects. `localStorage` keeps `sb.zen.music` (`"1"`/`"0"`, default `"0"`) and `sb.zen.track` (a file name; a missing or unknown one is the first track). A new track takes over with the same 6 s cross-fade.
- **Hidden page**: on a touch screen (`pointer: coarse`) the music pauses when the page is hidden and resumes when it is shown; on a desktop it keeps playing (O6). A paused element keeps its place.
- The music plays in every Zen screen, an item's included; it stops when the page leaves Zen (away) and resumes on coming back.

### Autoplay and unlocking on phones

- Browsers start an `AudioContext` suspended until a user gesture (iOS Safari, Chrome on Android, desktop Chrome under its autoplay policy). Zen creates its context at the first `pointerdown` or `keydown` on the page (capture, once) and resumes it there; before that, an effect that is due is dropped, never queued (a bowl that rings late is about nothing).
- The music starts only from a gesture: a tap on **♪**, or, when `sb.zen.music` is on from before, the first gesture anywhere on the page. Until then **♪** shows "Music: tap to play".
- iOS suspends the context when the page is hidden or the phone locks, and may refuse `resume()` without a gesture afterwards; when `resume()` fails or the context stays `suspended` or `interrupted`, Zen waits for the next gesture and **♪** says "tap to play" again.
- iOS mutes Web Audio under the ringer switch; Zen does not set `navigator.audioSession.type`, so the switch keeps working as the boss expects. The live check confirms both on the boss's iPhone.

### Music tracks

What the Suno worker delivers into `gui/sounds/zen/` (one commit, before step Z6):

- 3 tracks (O7), instrumental only, calm (drone, guqin or koto, shakuhachi or flute, soft bells), no vocals, no drums, nothing that rises suddenly.
- MP3, 44.1 kHz stereo, about 96 kb/s VBR, 2 to 3 minutes, each at most 2.5 MB and all at most 7.5 MB; integrated loudness about −20 LUFS (a quiet bed under the effects), no silence longer than 0.2 s at either end, tails that cross-fade cleanly into the start.
- File names `zen-<slug>.mp3`; `tracks.ts` with each file's English and Traditional Chinese title; `CREDITS.md` with, per track, the Suno account's plan, the creation date, the prompt, the Suno song id, and the licence statement that allows shipping it in an MIT npm package (O8). The README credits the music beside the icons' credit.

## Reminders, the tab icon, and the other views' sounds

- In Zen, `notify.signal` is not called for a snapshot (no Standard clip, no notification per arrival). Instead, when an item takes the screen while the page is hidden or unfocused, Zen calls a new `notify.remind(alert)`: the same system notification Standard would show for that alert, behind the same Remind switch and permission. Items that wait behind it raise none, so the notifications never hint at a queue either. Clicking the notification brings the page forward on that item.
- The tab icon carries no count while Zen is shown (the plain icon); away, Standard's badge comes back with the next snapshot.
- Standard's and Geek's `notify.ts` clips, switches, and notifications are unchanged.

## Wiring in `main.ts`

The changes to the page's coordinator, kept small and named here so the step's diff is reviewable:

- `zenOn()`: the resolved view is `zen`, the page is not away, and the route is not `settings`. `view.onChange` and `navigate` load the Zen chunk the first time `zenOn()` holds, and show or hide `#zen`.

## Risks

- **A view that hides things on purpose.** With no counts and no notices, the boss can miss a failure or a long queue while he sits in Zen. Standard is one Esc away and keeps everything; the tab icon's count comes back there. Whether failures should become items is O1.
- **iOS audio.** Media elements through `createMediaElementSource`, resuming after a lock, and the ringer switch have changed between iOS releases; the live check covers the boss's phone, and a failure leaves Zen silent, never stuck (**Autoplay**).
- **Track licence and size.** Shipping Suno tracks in an MIT npm package depends on the account's plan (O8); the package grows by several megabytes (O7).
- **Two views of one queue.** An item skipped in Zen is still first in Standard's queue, and an item answered in Standard leaves Zen; both read the same snapshots, so they never contradict each other about what waits, only about what is shown first.
- **The composer's wait.** An item that waits while the boss types in the companion could wait long if he leaves the box focused with a draft; it takes the screen as soon as the focus leaves the box (O10).

There is no headline when nothing waits: the idle circle carries one sourced mindfulness line (`gui/zenlines.ts`, 14 entries with their sources, shown in random order and fading every 45 s). The corner holds full screen (hidden where the browser has no Fullscreen API, with a one-time Home Screen hint on touch) and one menu (language, view, sound and volume through the settings page's own functions, and the way back to Standard); its music slot is hidden until Z6. The Zen chunk and stylesheet are over the budget above (about 15.6 KB raw JavaScript and 12.7 KB CSS); the figures are in `tests/imports.test.ts`.
