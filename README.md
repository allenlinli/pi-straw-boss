# pi-straw-boss

English | [繁體中文](https://github.com/wayne930242/pi-straw-boss/blob/main/README.zh-TW.md)

**Many pi agents, one project, no collisions.** pi-straw-boss gives the agents you run across [Herdr](https://herdr.dev) tabs a light chain of command: a main agent per tab, a tower per workspace, and a live view of who is doing what.

**[Try the browser view live](https://wayne930242.github.io/pi-straw-boss/)**: a demo with made-up agents that runs in your browser, nothing to install.

**Zen** is the third view, for the hours when you are answering the net rather than steering it. When something waits for you, the page shows that one thing alone: no map. A card never takes the screen by itself, whatever you are doing: one quiet line (above the input on the circle, under the name beside a session) says how many wait ("1 card waits for Enter. No rush, when you're ready."), and **Enter**, or a tap on the line, takes the next, blocking questions first. Enter then presses the card's main button (Allow, Trust, Handle, the first choice, Run), but never inside a text field. A card you are on is never replaced or pulled away: if its question is answered elsewhere it stays, says so (answered elsewhere, or that its unit left), and waits for you to close it. Nothing else takes the screen from you either: a terminal opened from a card stays until you go back, a session that ended or lost control stays with a quiet note (its box read-only, Send off), a changed server default view applies at the next load, and a new version of the page loads when you are back at the breath, not under you (one quiet line says it is ready). When you answer, cancel, or skip it (Maybe later), a breath of a pause follows and Zen breathes again; the next card waits for Enter. A wait a tower recorded on you (a question only you can settle) is a card of its own: **Handle** unfolds an answer box right on it, Enter or **Send** tells the tower in your words, and the card leaves with the knock; **Dismiss** drops the wait, and **Esc** folds the box with your draft kept. Four things Standard shows in the Link button, the Wi-Fi notice, and a tower's notices are cards of their own too: a **confirm** a teammate's tower asks of this computer (Allow or Deny, with the tool's full arguments), a **dialog a Straw Link box forwarded**, a **pair request** (type the number the phone shows, then Allow or Deny), and a **memory proposal** from a tower (Confirm or Reject). They answer through the same functions as Standard's rows, the server decides when one is over, and a paired phone keeps only the authority it has in Standard (no pair-request card: that is this computer's to decide). A join request stays in Standard. A dialog with several questions shows one at a time: the one you are on is open and clear, the others fold to one faded line (the question, and your answer once it has one), and a scroll or swipe, picking an answer, Tab, or a tap on a line moves on to the next. When nothing waits, a breathing circle and a short line on mindfulness or stillness, with whom it comes from (a Japanese source also has its original, small, below): every visit starts there. `[` or `]` steps to a session to keep you company, its live log and its composer, and the circle sits where the list wraps round; ⌘K's *Back to the breath* returns to it. No session, a tower included, is put there for you. One Esc cancels the question on the page, or skips the card when it cannot be cancelled; Esc twice (or the corner menu's last row) goes back to Standard. The corner menu also holds the language, the view, and the sound and volume, and the button beside it takes the page to full screen. An answer that leaves a terminal step (robot_hand's *Put it in my prompt* or a new Herdr pane) shows a small card in ink to finish it, which stays until you go back to Zen, with a quiet line when its step is done; *Full terminal* on the card opens the whole terminal inside Zen, with *Back to Zen* in the top right corner. **Keys.** Enter or Space ends the breathing pause early (so does a tap); Esc answers or skips the card on the page, and a second Esc within 600 ms goes back to Standard (with the corner menu open, Esc only closes it; with text in a box, it only lets the box go). Inside a terminal the page does not take Esc: Shift+Esc lets the keyboard out of the terminal. **H** opens *Context* on a card, the unit's session log inside the card, read-only, and you can still answer while it is open; H or Esc closes it. `[` and `]` step the session beside you (the companion) through the sessions you can talk to. **⌘K** (Ctrl+K off a Mac) opens a command palette for the page's actions and the corner menu's choices. While nothing waits, a quiet line at the top says how many units work and how many are idle; it never counts what waits. Moving the pointer leaves faint ripples. **Sound** follows the settings page's Sound switch (off by default) and Volume: a soft singing-bowl tone when an item takes the screen, a wooden-fish knock when one is done, a lower bowl on an error; the corner menu's *Try the sounds* plays them. Set it as the default with `/boss gui view zen`, or pick it on a device's settings page. Music is yours: put audio files (mp3, m4a, ogg, wav, flac, and the like) in `~/.pi/agent/straw-boss-music/` and Zen shows a ♪ in the corner that plays them on a loop, with a ⌄ to choose the track (shown when there are two or more); with no files there is no ♪. The folder is read each time Zen shows, and tracks cross-fade into themselves so the loop has no gap. On a phone nothing plays before your first tap (♪ says *tap to play* until then, and again after the phone locks), the music pauses while the page is hidden, and the ringer switch mutes it. Motion stops under *reduce motion*, and the page follows the system's forced colours.

## Why

Run a few agents on the same project and they start stepping on each other: two dev servers on one port, two branches editing the same migration, and no agent knows the others exist. pi-straw-boss lets them find each other and coordinate, without a scheduler or a new service.

- **Everyone joins.** Each pi in Herdr joins its tab's net at start as a member; nothing to ask or remember.
- **A main agent per tab, by promotion.** An agent whose work grows calls `boss_promote`, or is promoted when it first dispatches a subagent. A tab has one main, and nobody takes it over except you with `/boss`.
- **A tower per workspace.** It opens in its own tab, pinned first, when agents start to collide: a member dispatches a subagent (it promotes itself first), a workspace has two members while another workspace already has a tower, an agent promotes itself, or you type `/boss tower`. Mains and the tower go together: a tower that opens promotes each tab's earliest member, and a tower that quits turns every main back into a member (its subagents stay). It coordinates the main agents and the other towers, and opens a tab with a main agent for work no session owns. You don't need to talk to it.
- **A live dashboard** in the tower: who joined, and what they and their subagents are doing.
- **Towers talk to each other.** A tower lists every workspace's tower (`tower_list`: who a message for it reaches, what its tabs work on) and writes to one or all of them (`tower_send`); a workspace without a tower is reached through its mains, else its members. Every `call_tower`, `boss_broadcast`, and `tower_send` message opens with a header the code writes: who sent it, in what role, to whom, and whether a reply is expected ([how](docs/how-it-works.md#talking-between-towers)).
- **A head tower of your own.** One tower that belongs to no project: you talk to it in the GUI (its page, or the line under Zen's circle) or with `/boss head`, it keeps one long conversation with you across runs, and it hands each instruction to the tower of the workspace that owns it (`tower_send`, opening that tower if needed) and tells you in a line when the tower answers. Its tools are the tower's lookup and forwarding tools and nothing else, and it listens only to you and to answers to its own forwards ([how](docs/how-it-works.md#the-head-tower)).
- **A work board.** A main's board fills itself from its todo list and its name (`board_set` overrides it); allies see only an auto entry's name and progress (`<name> (3/5)`), never its task text; towers read every main's entry (`board_read`), on this computer and, with Straw Link, on teammates' computers, so a tower routes work knowing where each main stands ([how](docs/how-it-works.md#the-work-board)).
- **A tower closes the mains it opened.** A main a tower opened for a request reports to the sender and to the tower, and the tower closes it (`boss_close_main`) when nothing is running in it ([how](docs/how-it-works.md#closing-an-opened-main)).
- **A merge train per project.** A project that turns it on in `apps.json` has one integrator main take its coordinators' branches as cars. The integrator merges a train of cars together, has the combined result reviewed once, and runs the heavy gates (lint, build, full suites) once per train instead of once per branch. Where the app lists the stages, it goes on to deploy the train (to an `auto` target on the config's standing word, to a `strict` one only on your words for it) and opens one review page per train for your acceptance; a car you comment on goes back to its coordinator. Straw Boss sets the flow and the project's skill holds every concrete check ([how](docs/how-it-works.md#merge-train)). To run without it on a computer, set `STRAW_BOSS_INTEGRATION=off` in the environment of `herdr server` and its sessions, or `"integration": "off"` in `<agentDir>/straw-boss/config.json` (`<agentDir>` is `PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR`, else `~/.pi/agent`; the variable wins; a setting that cannot be read is off, with a notice; under the desk, `STRAW_BOSS_DESK=1`, it is off unless you set it on): no main or tower then takes the integrator role, a merge or a deploy waits for your word, and `integration` in `apps.json` is ignored.
- **A digest for the tower**: joins, subagent launches, and renames in its workspace since its last run, read into its next run once and cleared ([how](docs/how-it-works.md#the-towers-digest)).
- **A browser view** of the whole net, `/boss gui`: every workspace as a base with its tower, agents, and subagents, animated and read-only. Click a base to zoom in, click a unit to read its chat as a live comms log; units that wait on you queue up as alerts, and Space opens the next one, F enlarges the top dialog, and Escape closes it (none of them while you type); a question is answered from the keyboard too: arrows choose, Tab walks a question's own-words box and note and then on to the next question, and Enter confirms and goes on. A switch in the header's settings (the gear) raises a system notification, and another plays a sound, when a unit waits on you, fails, or finishes; finished units wait in a list of their own, cleared one by one or all at once, and a finished subagent on the map is dismissed one by one or all at once (Dismiss finished, beside the location). A unit's comms show its background monitors, and one that fails or goes quiet raises a notice (a quiet entry under the header's Notices button; only a unit waiting on you raises the prominent alert); a unit that is idle while a subagent of it works, or while a monitor of it runs, shows as commanding with a glow, and one that only waits on a running monitor shows its note in red. On a desktop a unit's terminal takes your keyboard once you click into it. An answer that leaves its next step in a terminal (robot_hand's prompt and pane choices) shows a small card to finish it: the command, a pane's last rows, one line (hidden while a password is asked), and one button, with a link to the full terminal for ssh and vim. It reads in English or Traditional Chinese, following your browser, with a language menu in the header's settings. + New base, the last card on the map, opens a base in a folder you pick with one member and no tower. A base without a tower takes more members (+ Member on the base adds one in a new tab, on a squad beside that squad), and its tower opens by the rules above; a base with a tower takes new mains (+ Main, on the base or in the tower's comms), each in a tab of its own. In a session's composer, a file pasted, dropped, or picked with + (20 MB at most) is stored in a private temp folder and its path goes into the message. × closes a base, a squad, or (in its view) a unit after a confirmation: each agent quits pi as `/quit` would, then its pane, tab, or workspace closes; nothing closes if one of them refuses. Each opening form takes an optional first message; the folder is picked in Finder-style columns (with New folder, and the base named after the folder by default), staying inside your home folder and skipping hidden folders (on Windows also the ones with the Hidden or System attribute, such as AppData and the old profile links), and while the unit opens a deploy panel shows the real steps (create, start, join the net, deliver the first message) with each step's clock, and names the step and the reason when one fails. A pi that must confirm project trust before it starts (a folder with `.pi/` settings, extensions, skills, or MCP servers) asks in the GUI as an alert too, with the folder, what trusting would load, and Trust, Trust this time, or Don't trust (the terminal can answer as well, and the first answer counts; a paired phone may answer; the deploy panel of an opening shows it inline).
- **An unattended device**, a computer nobody sits at (a build box, a server), takes work from teammates' computers over Straw Link: it runs the sb server as the link holder, with no boot pi session, and a controller invites it from the GUI ([setup](docs/unattended-device.md), also [in Traditional Chinese](docs/unattended-device.zh-TW.md)). On a box several developers share, a unit's questions and notices go only to the controller that dispatched it and to the team's admins, and the work of a device that leaves the team or loses `dispatch` is ended.
- **Broadcast** to a tab, a workspace, every tower (a workspace without one, through its mains or members), or every session.
- **Tidy tabs.** When a pi starts in a tab or quits out of it, the tab's panes are made equal again, so workers split off one by one don't leave a chain of halves.
- **Stranded subagents are asked to report.** A subagent that stays open after finishing, while the pi that launched it was restarted and no longer receives its result, is asked once over intercom to send its final report there ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#orphaned-subagents)).
- **Subagents a Herdr restart brought back as plain pi sessions are resumed.** A restart (`resume_agents_on_restore`) starts a plain `pi --session` in each subagent's pane, without what makes it a child, so its result never arrives. Straw Boss closes that restored pane and asks the launching session to resume the run with pi-herdr-agents' `subagent_resume`; a managed-worktree run keeps its pane and is reported instead; a child whose turn the restart cut off is told once to check the state and continue ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#subagents-a-herdr-restore-brought-back-plain)).
- **A net view in Herdr's sidebar.** An optional plugin turns the Agents list into the net, towers first and each subagent under its launcher, on by default and switched by one key.
- **A reaper for leftover worktree panes.** A second optional plugin closes the panes agents leave behind in worktree workspaces, removes the clean checkouts nothing holds, and keeps the workspaces you pin.

## Install

```bash
pi install npm:pi-straw-boss
```

### Requirements

- pi running inside [Herdr](https://herdr.dev) 0.9.4 or newer.
- Herdr's pi integration (`herdr integration install pi`): without it Herdr does not name a pane's pi session, and the session cannot join the net; each session warns once, 20 s after its start, when it is missing.
- [pi-intercom](https://www.npmjs.com/package/pi-intercom) 0.18.1 or newer: the net's channels, messages, and the GUI's dialog relay run over it, and so does the sb server's connection (it checks the version at start and says so in `start-error.json` and `boss_server status` when pi-intercom is older or missing).
- [pi-anchorloop](https://www.npmjs.com/package/pi-anchorloop) 2.10.1 or newer: the merge train's procedure is its `integrating` (merge) and `deploying` (deploy, UAT) skills, and Straw Boss's prompts bind the places those skills leave open ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#merge-train)). Without it a main or integrator that meets a car slip reports `blocked` naming pi-anchorloop and merges nothing; with the merge train off it is not used.
- Windows support is experimental: the extension reaches Herdr over its named pipe there, the sidebar plugin runs with the `node` on `PATH`, the worktree reaper is macOS and Linux only, and the project-trust relay works only while pi's agent folder lies inside your user profile (elsewhere pi asks in its terminal as before) ([what differs](docs/how-it-works.md#windows)).

## Quick start

1. Open pi in a Herdr tab and give it work. It joins the net by itself; when its work grows it promotes itself to the tab's main agent, which also opens the workspace tower. You can take over with `/boss`.
2. Type `/boss status` to see each tab's main agent and the towers.
3. Type `/boss tower` to open the workspace's tower and watch its dashboard. It also opens by itself when agents start to collide.
4. Type `/boss gui` from any session to see the whole net in your browser.

The tower only routes and coordinates, so it can run a lighter model: set `towerModel` in `~/.pi/agent/straw-boss/config.json` ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#how-it-works)). The tower service of the sb server is on by default: the tower is a run that wakes for the events it gets and ends with a handoff note (`/boss tower <prompt>` writes to it), and a teammate's Straw Link message gets an ally run of its own. Each run starts fresh; what has to outlive it (your standing instructions and decisions, and the tower's open waits) sits in a small memory you read and change with `/boss memory`, or in the tower's comms in the GUI; the tower can only propose a standing instruction or a decision, and it takes effect when you confirm it ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#tower-memory)). Set `{ "towerRuns": { "enabled": false } }` there to keep resident towers ([how it works](docs/how-it-works.md#the-tower-service-b4)).

## Tools and commands

| Name | What it does |
|---|---|
| `boss_promote` | Become this tab's main agent (refused while it has one). |
| `boss_status` | Main agents, this workspace's tower, and the other towers. |
| `call_tower` | Message this workspace's tower, or another workspace's (under a header). `why` says milestone, shared, or blocked: the three points a main calls it; `reply: false` for a notice. |
| `boss_open_main` | Tower only: open a tab with a new main agent for a request no session owns. |
| `boss_close_main` | Tower only: close a main this tower opened, once nothing runs in it. |
| `tower_list` | Tower only: every workspace's tower, who a message for it reaches, its state, and what its tabs work on. |
| `tower_send` | Tower only: message one other tower (by workspace id or label), or all of them. |
| `board_read` | Tower only: the work board: what each main is doing and how far it is, here and on teammates' computers. |
| `board_set` | A tab's main: override its own board entry (one line of current work, up to 8 checklist items), `auto: true` to give it back to the auto-fill from its todo list and name, or `clear: true` to delete it when its work is finished or shelved. |
| `boss_broadcast` | Message a tab, a workspace, all towers, or every session (under a header). |
| `boss_server` | See the sb server (status works with the server down), read its log (up to 200 lines), or restart it with a reason. Refused while the boss stopped the server, once a minute at most, and never in a remote turn. |

## Net view in the sidebar

An optional Herdr plugin in [`herdr-plugin/`](herdr-plugin) turns the sidebar's Agents list into a net view.
It lists the head tower's workspace first (once the head tower exists), in each workspace the tower, then each tab's main agent and members, and every subagent right under the session that launched it, even in a worktree workspace.
The view is on whenever the Herdr server starts, and one key switches it off and on again.
pi-straw-boss writes the sort key (`sb_order`), a role label (`sb_mark`) and, on subagents, a tree branch (`sb_indent`) and the subagent's name (`sb_name`) the view and your sidebar rows can use.
It needs Herdr 0.9.5 or newer and node.

<p align="center"><sub>The net view with each agent's role mark. <a href="docs/how-it-works.md#sidebar-net-view">How it works</a> has the sidebar rows that show them.</sub></p>

Link it from a checkout, then bind a key in Herdr's `config.toml`:

```bash
herdr plugin link /path/to/pi-straw-boss/herdr-plugin
```

```toml
[[keys.command]]
key = "prefix+alt+n"
type = "plugin_action"
command = "wayne930242.straw-boss.net"

```

On Windows the action is `wayne930242.straw-boss.net-windows` (Herdr wants an action id unique across platforms).
Run `herdr server reload-config` to load the key, and `herdr plugin unlink wayne930242.straw-boss` to remove the plugin.

## Leftover worktree panes

A second optional Herdr plugin, in [`herdr-reaper/`](herdr-reaper), cleans up after agents that ran in worktree workspaces.
In a workspace whose worktree is a linked git worktree (whatever its name), it closes the pane of an agent that has exited once the pane's shell is idle, and never the focused pane.
Herdr then closes the tab and the workspace, and the plugin removes the workspace's checkout if `git status` is clean and nothing else is using it (no other pane works in it, no other workspace is open on it).
A worktree that pi-herdr-agents created is kept until its parent session removes it with `worktree_remove`, for as long as the parent session is open in a Herdr pane (or, if it is not, until it has been silent for 72 hours, `retain_idle_hours`), and so is any checkout a process of yours has as its working directory.
Git-ignored files (`.env`, `node_modules`, caches, local databases) do not make a checkout dirty, so they are deleted with it.
The branch always stays, and so does a dirty checkout, with the reason in the plugin log.
A checkout with initialized submodules is removed only when every submodule (nested ones too) has no uncommitted or untracked paths and its HEAD is contained in a remote-tracking ref (or in a ref of the main repository's own copy of the module), so no commit is lost; the reaper then runs `git submodule deinit --all`, deletes the worktree's `.git/worktrees/<id>/modules` (which git refuses to remove the worktree around), and runs a plain `git worktree remove`. Otherwise it stays and the log names the submodule and why; if a removal fails partway and leaves files git no longer tracks, the log says "half-removed" and names the directory for you to delete.
When Herdr opened a plain shell workspace for the main checkout to group the worktrees under, the plugin remembers it, and closes it once no worktree workspace of that repository is left and it holds only idle shells (no agent, not pinned, not renamed, not focused); the checkout and branches are untouched.
Every other workspace, including a main-checkout workspace that never had a worktree under it, is left alone.
It sweeps every 10 minutes and a minute after an agent exits, and the `reap-now` action sweeps at once.
Every close, removal, skip and pin is a line in the plugin log (`herdr plugin log list --plugin wayne930242.straw-boss-reaper`).

A pinned workspace is never reaped and its checkout is never removed; that is all a pin does. Closing a pinned workspace closes it.
Renaming a workspace pins it at once, and the `pin` action toggles the pin of the workspace it runs in; unpinning leaves the name as it is.
The pin is the workspace token `$pin`, a Nerd Font pin glyph, which a Space row shows once you add it.
It needs Herdr 0.9.3 or newer, git and node 18 or newer.
It runs on macOS and Linux only: on Windows, Herdr does not report what a pane's shell is running, so the reaper could not tell an idle pane from one running a build or an editor.

```bash
herdr plugin install wayne930242/pi-straw-boss/herdr-reaper
```

```toml
[[keys.command]]
key = "prefix+i"
type = "plugin_action"
command = "wayne930242.straw-boss-reaper.pin"
description = "pin or unpin the workspace"

[ui.sidebar.spaces]
rows = [["state_icon", "workspace", { token = "$pin" }], ["branch", "git_status"]]
```

`herdr plugin link /path/to/pi-straw-boss/herdr-reaper` links a checkout instead, and `herdr plugin unlink wayne930242.straw-boss-reaper` removes the plugin.
The plugin starts reaping as soon as it is installed or linked, with no trial run and no confirmation: its first sweep treats every idle, agent-less pane in a linked-worktree workspace as one whose agent exited, about a minute and a half after the first hook runs.
Run `bash herdr-reaper/run.sh reaper.mjs dry-run` first to see what it would do, and pin the workspaces you want to keep (the pin action works once the plugin is linked, and a `config.json` with `only_under` can be put in the plugin's config directory before that).
[How it works](docs/how-it-works.md#worktree-reaper) has the rules and the limits.

## Process hygiene

Every pi loads a small extension that marks its environment, so what a worker's bash starts carries the worker's id even when `timeout` or `setsid` moved it to a process group of its own.
A pi-herdr-agents worker registers with the sb server; when the worker quits or its pane closes, the server stops what it left (SIGTERM, then SIGKILL after 10 s, each step checked by pid and start time) and logs one `procs-reaped` line.
`worktree_remove` and a bash `git worktree remove` are refused while a process works in the checkout, with the pid, program name, CPU and age of each holder and the command that reaps them: `node dist/server/cli.mjs procs reap --checkout <path>`.
`cli.mjs procs` lists the workers the server knows.
Heavy-command slots, Linux cgroups and a runaway-process watchdog are planned for a later release.
[How it works](docs/how-it-works.md#process-hygiene-and-slots) has the rules and the limits.
