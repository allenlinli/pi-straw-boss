## Intent

The GUI has two views today: Standard (the Tokyo Night map with its motion) and Geek (black, monospace, still).
Both show the whole net at once: every base, the HUD alert with its count, the dock of dialogs, the notices.
The boss wants a third view, **Zen**, for the hours when he is not steering the net but answering it:

- 「先處理待辦，沒事時陪著一個 session」: when something waits for him, the whole page shows only that one item; after he answers, cancels, or skips it, the next one; when nothing waits, the page shows the session he last talked to, its live log and its composer. Esc twice, or a small control in a corner, returns to the Standard view (one Esc answers the card on the page: **Esc in Zen**).
- 「不用那麼嚴格，只要不要顯示待辦數量或是還有東西需要處理」: the page never shows how many items wait, nor any hint that more are queued: no counts, no dots, no "also queued". Other numbers may stay where they help. **(Z-D19, 2026-11-06: the one exception is the cue above the head strip's input, whose wording the boss gave with a count.)**
- 「呼吸圈＋三秒停頓，墨色」: a breathing circle (inhale 4 s, exhale 6 s) when nothing waits, a very slow drift of the background colour, and after an item is handled a 3 s breathing pause before the next one (a tap skips it). The palette is "ink": a warm black ground, rice-paper off-white text, few accents. Dark only.
- Sound: background music from tracks the boss generates with Suno (a separate worker produces them into `gui/sounds/zen/`), looped with a crossfade, off by default, on and off with one tap, with a choice of track; effects synthesized with Web Audio: a soft singing-bowl tone when a new item takes the screen, a wooden-fish (木魚) knock when one is done, a lower bowl on an error. The volume follows the settings page's Volume. The sounds of Standard and Geek stay as they are. **(As built in Z6: the tracks are the boss's own files in a server-served music folder; see Music.)**
- Zen is a view like the other two: `/boss gui view zen` sets the server's default, and each device can choose it on the settings page.

## Scope

- In: the view's place beside Standard and Geek (the device's choice, the server's default, `gui.json`); which waiting things become Zen items, their order, and what each item shows and how it is answered; the companion session and its fallbacks; the pause, the skip, and leaving and coming back; motion, colour, and the calm rules; the effects and the music, with the phones' autoplay rules; the asset budget; reminders and the tab icon in Zen; both languages; the demo states, screenshots, and the GIF; the build steps, tests, and docs.
- Out: any change to Standard or Geek beyond the fourth button of the view control, the header's way back into Zen, and moving `intel` out of `layer.ts` (**Design**); any new server route or any change to what a paired device may do; a light variant; a Zen in the terminal (the tower dashboard, the sidebar); producing the music (the Suno worker's task; this spec states what it must deliver, **Music tracks**); the desk shell's own colour for `zen` (straw-boss-desk; **Docs to change** names the follow-up).

## Terms

- **Item**: one thing that waits for the boss, as Zen shows it: a unit that waits on an answer (mark `waiting`, or a dialog pending) or a project trust question. Exactly the alerts of rank 0 (`rankOf`, `gui/alerts.ts:26`), the set the HUD alert button serves in Standard, plus Zen's link cards (**Link cards**). An item is either **blocking** or not (**Order**, Z-D18): a blocking one holds an agent that cannot go on until the boss answers.
- **Current item**: the one item on the screen. There is at most one.
- **Resting item**: an item the boss skipped; it cannot become current until its rest ends (**Skip and rest**).
- **Companion**: the session Zen shows while no item is current (**The companion**).
- **Pause**: the 3 s breath between an item leaving the screen and what comes next.
- **Away**: this page left Zen for Standard (Esc twice, the corner control, or the settings page) without changing the device's choice (**Leaving and coming back**).
- **Effects**: the three synthesized sounds (bowl, knock, low bowl). **Music**: the looped Suno tracks. **(As built in Z6: the tracks are the boss's own files in a server-served music folder; see Music.)**

## Design in one page

```text
<html data-view="zen">                       one layer over the whole page; Standard's DOM is display:none under it
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ [Standard]                                        [♪] [⌄]    │  corner controls only, 44 px, quiet until hovered or focused
│                                                              │
│                ( item on the screen )                        │  an item: who asks, the question, its answer card,
│        ops-main · Harbor API                                 │           Send / Cancel / Not now — nothing about others
│        "Robot Hand — Deploy needs your sudo password"        │
│        ( ) Type it into a new pane   ( ) Put it in my prompt │
│        [Send answer]  [Cancel]  [Not now]                    │
│                                                              │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
answered → knock → outcome 1.2 s → pause 3 s (one breath; a tap skips) → next item, or:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ [Standard]                                        [♪] [⌄]    │
│                         ◯  (breathes 4 s in, 6 s out)        │  the circle behind the log, faint
│   tower · Harbor API                                         │  the companion: the session he last talked to
│   … newest entries of its log, live …                        │
│   [ composer                                         Send ]  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Zen is a value of the existing view: `zen` beside `standard` and `geek`, for the device and for the server. Nothing on the server changes but the accepted value.
- The Zen layer is a chunk of its own (`gui/zen.ts` with `gui/zen.css`), loaded only on a page whose view is Zen. It reads the same snapshots, alerts, and dialogs as Standard and answers through the same cards and routes; it adds no server route.
- A pure model (`gui/zenmodel.ts`) decides what is on the screen: the current item, resting items, the pause, the companion. The DOM follows it, and the sounds follow its transitions, so the rules are tested without a browser, as `overlay.ts` is.
- Standard keeps running underneath as it does for a hidden page: it holds the newest snapshot and draws it when the boss leaves Zen, so leaving shows the net as it is.

## The view

### Choice, default, and record

- `Choice` becomes `"auto" | "standard" | "geek" | "zen"` and `View` `"standard" | "geek" | "zen"` (`gui/view.ts`); `choiceOf` accepts `zen`; `THEME` gets `zen`, the ink ground (**Ink**), so the browser's bar and an installed app's bar match.
- Auto stays as it is: it follows the server's default when one is set, else Standard, or Geek when the screen asks for more contrast. Auto never picks Zen by itself; only the server's default or the device's choice does. A server default of `zen` wins over `prefers-contrast: more`, as `geek` and `standard` do today; Zen's text meets 7 : 1 (**Ink**), so a device that asks for contrast is still served.
- The settings page's view control gets a fourth button, **Zen** (`data-choice="zen"`), and the Auto button reads **Follow server (Zen)** when the server's default is `zen`. The four buttons wrap on a phone the way the three do with `data-following`.
- `VIEWS` in `src/server/guiprefs.ts` gets `zen`; `gui-view` accepts it, and its refusal reads "The default view is auto, standard, geek, or zen"; `GuiRecord.view` may be `"zen"`; `src/host.ts` names it in its words (`Zen`); `BOSS_ARGUMENTS` gets `gui view zen`: "Default view: Zen (one waiting item at a time, then a session for company) for devices that follow the server".
- `/boss gui view zen` therefore needs no new path: the control socket stores it, every page gets it in its meta, hello, and `view` event, and a page that follows the server switches at once. A page of an older build that hears `zen` reads it as Auto (`choiceOf`), and a stream from another build reloads the page anyway (`rebuilt`, `gui/main.ts`).

### What is hidden, and the first frame

- Under `[data-view="zen"]` the main stylesheet hides every part of Standard's page (`display: none` on the stage, header, HUD, dock, tiles, timeline, banner, drawer) and shows `#zen`, an empty `<section id="zen" hidden>` in `index.html` that the chunk fills (named by `aria-label`; the page's `<main>` stays the map's). The first-frame block (ground, circle, hiding) is in `style.css`, about 1 KB, so a page that opens in Zen paints ink from its first frame with no flash of Standard; everything else is in `gui/zen.css`, loaded with the chunk.
- The connection overlay (`gui/connect.ts`) still covers the page while there is no live state, in Zen as in the other views; `zen.css` gives it the ink colours.
- In Zen, the hotkeys of the dialogs (Space, F) do nothing, the phone drawer and its swipe do not act, and the tab icon shows no count (`badge` draws the plain icon while Zen is shown).

### Leaving and coming back

- **H** (Z4d) expands or collapses the shown item's context (below). It is a row of Zen's keymap (`KEYMAP.zen`, written by `gui/zenrows.ts`) and acts only on a card with a context link: not while the corner menu or the terminal layer is open, not on the pause or the companion.
- **Esc in Zen** is one table (`gui/zenkeys.ts`, `escStep`; the layer gathers the facts and acts on the answer, `escape` in `gui/zen.ts`), taken in the capture phase before any field's own handler (`main.ts`), never going up a route. In this order:
  1. the corner menu is open: Esc closes it and does nothing else;
  2. the item's context (Z4d, below) is open: Esc collapses it, the focus going back to its 前情 link, and does nothing else;
  3. a text field of the layer has the focus and has text in it, on any screen: Esc only blurs it (the text stays);
  4. an item is on the page and can still be answered: **one Esc cancels its question when it is cancellable** (the card's own Cancel, pressed: `mountAsk`'s `cancel()`, so the same body and the same outcome), else it is **Not now** (the card rests, the pause starts). It never leaves Zen: the next card gets the next Esc. A card whose answer is on its way or whose outcome shows takes no Esc as an answer;
  5. an empty field has the focus on the companion's screen, the pause, or the empty state: Esc only lets go of it;
  5a. (0.59.5, head tower strip) the head tower's lines are clear under the circle: Esc sends them away, the mindfulness line comes back, and nothing else happens (boss decision 2026-11-05, option ①; `docs/specs/2026-11-04-head-tower.md`, **Resting the strip**). A strip that has already faded to its rest level has nothing to send away and does not take the key;
  6. nothing above took the key: the first such Esc shows a quiet line at the foot of the page, 「再按一次 Esc 回到標準」 / "Press Esc again for Standard"; a second one within **600 ms** leaves (away, below). The line fades with the window (about 1 s in all) and a later Esc starts again. On the companion, the empty state, and the pause one Esc therefore does nothing but show the line.
  A key held down and repeating is no second press (it changes nothing). A modal (`MODAL`) and any handler that called `preventDefault` before the layer still take the key first. The corner control's title and the menu's way back read 「回到標準畫面（Esc Esc）」.
- The **corner control** (reading **Standard**, in the menu) leaves Zen at once on any screen, a phone's included.
- Leaving is **away** for this page only: `data-view` becomes `standard`, `<html>` gets `data-zen-away`, the device's choice stays `zen`, and nothing is stored. Standard shows the newest snapshot (the hidden-page path of `show`), and:
  - with an item on the screen, the page opens that item's dialog on top of the dock (`stack.show(alert)`), so the boss goes on with it in Standard;
  - with the companion on the screen, the page goes to the companion's session route;
  - during a pause, the route the page is on.
- **Coming back** (`face.show`, 0.59.5): Zen draws the newest snapshot, and an item the model still held on the screen from before the page left, which the snapshot no longer lists (a forwarded question the boss answered in the ally base's panel and the link then closed, a unit he answered in its dialog), is dropped at once: the circle shows, with no card, no outcome line, and no knock, because the boss answered it himself while Zen was not there to say so. An item the snapshot still lists is still the screen and is not announced a second time. A question that is settled while Zen is shown is as it was: the knock, the line *Already answered elsewhere*, then the pause (`model.update(alerts, returning)`, `gui/zenmodel.ts`). Before this, the card of the closed question came back with *Already answered elsewhere* for 1.2 s (the trial of 2026-11-05, box trial12: `waydosoft asks you` for a dialog that was already closed).
- While away, Standard's header shows a **Zen** button (beside the gear) that returns to Zen; it is hidden whenever the device's resolved view is not Zen. A reload, or a new page of this device, opens in Zen again.

### Choosing a view

A view is something one switches into, so **a choice shows at once, from anywhere**: on the settings page, in Zen's corner menu, or on another page of this device. One function decides what a move does to the page's face (`faceAfter` and `mountFace`, `gui/viewface.ts`; `tests/viewface.test.ts` walks every start state: the device's choice, the server's default, away or not, on the settings route or not):

| Move | What shows |
|---|---|
| **Pick** (the settings page's four buttons; Zen's menu: Auto, Standard, Geek, Zen; another page of the device) | The view the choice resolves to, immediately: the away mark is cleared, so a Zen pick brings Zen back even when this page had left it, and **a Zen pick on the settings page leaves the settings page** (Back, as its own Back does). Standard and Geek leave Zen at once. Auto follows the server's default now (or the screen's contrast). Picking the view that is already chosen still applies it. |
| **Follow** (the server's default changed while the choice is Auto; the OS's contrast changed) | The new view at once, except that a Zen arriving while the settings page is open waits there (away) until the page is left: the settings page is never covered by something the boss did not pick; and a page that is **showing Zen is never switched out of it** by a new default (Z-D20, R3): it applies at the next load, or when the page leaves Zen. |
| **Esc Esc, the corner control, an item's or a session's exit** | This page shows Standard; the device's choice stays. |
| **The header's Zen button** | Zen shows again. |
| **The route** moves onto the settings page | A Zen device's page is Standard on it (away), and shows Zen on leaving it; other views are not touched. |

The view control (`view.ts`) tells its listeners every pick, even one that resolves to the view already shown, and a follow only when the view changed; the page's face therefore never depends on whether the resolved view moved.

## Items

### Which kinds

- An item is an alert of rank 0 from `alertsOf`: a unit (tower, main, member, subagent, outside unit) whose mark is `waiting` or that has a dialog pending, and a project trust question. It is the same set Standard's HUD alert serves, so the two views never disagree about what waits. Which of them blocks an agent, and so goes first, is the table under **Order**.
- Not items: a unit that failed, a monitor that failed or went quiet, a unit that finished (rank 1). Standard keeps them in its Notices; Zen shows none of them (O1). A tower's wait for the boss is an item since 0.58.0 (`gui/waits.ts`); the four questions of **Link cards** below are items since 0.58.3. The head tower's own questions are not (HT-D3), and the Link notices that only inform (an inbox note, a held message, a fork, a mismatch, an unreachable relay, an unattended device's notice) are not.
- An opening this page runs (`ownOpenings`) is not an alert and so not an item.

### Order

- The next item is the eligible one (not resting) of the highest rank, and within a rank the one that has waited longest: by the unit's `since` (the time it took its present state, the same on every device), or for a trust question the earliest `at` of its agents; an item without either takes the time this page first saw it. Ties go by key. There are two ranks, **blocking** first (Z-D18, below).
- A current item stays until it leaves (**When an item leaves**): an older item that appears meanwhile (a page that just connected, a unit that started waiting while the stream was down), and a blocking question too, never replaces it (Z-D20: a card is never replaced or pulled away; **Blocking first** below only says which card Enter takes next).
- An item that arrives while another is current, or during a pause, makes no sound, no motion, and no change on the screen. It waits in the queue and the cue counts it; Enter takes it when the boss is ready.

#### Blocking first (Z-D18)

Found in the 2026-11-06 box trial (trial14, 0.59.5): Zen showed the MP Center tower's wait (*MP-4219: Handle, Dismiss, Maybe later*) when a box's forwarded question arrived, kept the wait, and left the question behind it with nothing on the page to say so. A question that holds an agent hangs it for as long as the boss does not answer, and a tower's notice holds no one. Boss decision 2026-11-06, 「Blocking first; untouched cards yield」: a blocking question outranks a notice-type item, and takes the screen from one that the boss has not touched. `blocks(alert)` in `gui/zenmodel.ts` is the one place that ranks.

Every kind of item, ranked by whether an agent's work stops until the boss answers:

| Kind of item (`alert`) | Rank | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A unit that waits or has a dialog pending (`Unit.pending`, mark `waiting`: main, member, subagent, outside unit, a tower's own dialog), a pane blocked on a terminal prompt included | **Blocking** | The agent's tool call is that dialog, or its pane is stopped on that prompt. |
| A project trust question (`trust`) | **Blocking** | The folder's agents wait on it (`gui/alerts.ts`: "the folder's agents wait on it"). |
| A question a Straw Link box forwarded (`card.kind` `dialog`) | **Blocking** | The remote agent's dialog is open until it is answered; nothing on the box can answer it. This is the case of the trial. |
| A teammate's tower asked this computer to allow a call (`card.kind` `confirm`) | **Blocking** | The calling session's tool call waits for the answer for up to ten minutes (`CONFIRM_MAX_MS`, `src/link/holder.ts`) and is refused when none comes. Boss ruling 2026-11-06: classed as blocking, not with the notices. |
| A wait the tower recorded on the boss (`wait`) | Lower | A note in the tower's memory: the tower goes on with other work, and the boss may answer in a day. |
| A pair request (`card.kind` `pair`) | Lower | A device waits to be paired; no agent's call waits. |
| A memory proposal of a tower run (`card.kind` `proposal`) | Lower | The memory is unchanged until it is confirmed; no agent waits. |

Not items, as before: a unit that failed or finished, a failed monitor, a join request, the Link notices that only inform.

- **(Superseded by Z-D19, 2026-11-06: no card takes the screen by itself on the breathing circle, so nothing is preempted; the order below says which comes next.) A blocking question takes the screen** from a lower-ranked card that the boss has not touched, with the bowl, as any item does when it arrives. The card that was on the screen is not lost and is not Later: it is not put aside, stays eligible in its place by age, and comes back by itself after the question's answer and the 3 s pause, since it is then the next eligible item (or after a longer wait, if other blocking questions come first). It comes back **as it was**: the page keeps the yielded card (`parked` in `gui/zen.ts`) with its draft and its open reply box, and shows it again instead of making it again; it is dropped when its item leaves the list or its question changes (`cardSig`), and when Zen is left (the draft goes with the visit). No count and no hint that it exists (Z-D15 stays: one focus).
- **(Superseded by Z-D20: a card is never replaced by another, the unit's next question included, so there is nothing to make inert; `inert()` and Esc's inert guard are gone.) The card that replaces another is inert** (ZBF-F001, review of the first version; it stayed under Z-D19 for a card replaced by the unit's next question, the one case left where one card took the screen from another): the old card stays on the page for 1.3 s, fading (`zen-ink-out`, `gui/zen.css`), and a tap or a key the boss aimed at it must not land on the question that replaced it (a Dismiss pressed on a wait would otherwise be a Run it now, an Allow, or a Trust). Until the fade ends the new card has `inert` (no pointer, no key, no focus; Esc does not put it aside as Maybe later) and takes no focus; then it is live and takes the focus as any card does. Under reduced motion the old card is gone at once and the window is 0.5 s. This holds for any card drawn over one that is still fading (a preempt, or a dialog swapped for another under the same alert); the first card, and the one after the pause, are live at once.
- **(Superseded by Z-D19: a card on the screen is never interrupted, so `gui/zentouch.ts`, `watchTouch`, the parked card, and the 500 ms look are gone.) A card the boss is touching is not interrupted, and a card the boss walked away from is.** Boss ruling 2026-11-06 (ZBF-F002), 「Touched expires after 20 s; draft kept」: the card *is touched* (`gui/zentouch.ts`, `watchTouch`) when its answer is on its way (`sending`), or when a tap, touch, key, wheel, or input reached the card within the last **20 s** (`DRAFT_MS`, the draft guard's window under **What it shows**, the same as the companion's; this also covers a field the boss focused and a pick). ### Several questions

A dialog with two questions or more (an `ask_user_question` questionnaire, or the read-only list of several) is not stacked on the card in Zen; Standard and Geek still stack it. The boss's rule: one question at a time (several may show, but one is focused and the rest fade), and moving to the next is a scroll. `gui/zenquestions.ts` puts the answer panel's question blocks into one column (`.zen-qs`):

- **One focused, the rest one line.** The focused block is open and at full strength. Every other block is folded to one line, its summary (a button, `.zen-sum`): the header (or the question when it has none), and ` · ` and the answer once it has one (the chosen options' titles, or the own words cut to 32 characters), at 0.55 opacity (0.6 and a dashed border under forced colours). The lines above and below the open block are therefore always in view, at every size, and show the answer the boss left. A folded block's controls are not drawn (`display: none`), so neither a pointer nor a screen reader's activation can reach them: the line is the only thing that answers, and a tap, Enter, or Space on it, or Tab onto it, brings that block in and puts the keyboard focus inside it.
- **Moving on is paging, not free scrolling.** The column never scrolls. One wheel gesture (a notch, or a trackpad's push) moves one question: wheel events less than 100 ms apart are one gesture, a gesture moves once its travel passes 24 px, and the inertia that follows (events closer than 100 ms to the last) moves nothing, so one fling is one move; a pause of 100 ms starts a new gesture. A swipe of more than 40 px, or a quick flick (16 px or more, 0.3 px/ms or faster), moves one question, up for the next. The focused block's own scroll takes the wheel first and the swipe too: while it can scroll the way the wheel or the finger goes it does (a wheel over a folded line scrolls it for the browser), and the gesture that began in it ends in it; the next gesture at its end moves on. At the first and last question a gesture moves nothing and the page behind does not move either. A pointer's pick of a single choice (the next question without an answer, after a 240 ms beat so the pick is seen; a multiple choice, an arrow key's pick, and own words do not move it), Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter (the keys of `askkeys.ts`), and a tap on a line move too. The keyboard focus goes with a move when it was in a question.
- **A taller question scrolls inside itself.** A focused block taller than the room it has scrolls in its own box and always opens at its top (its header and its question, never above the view), whichever way it came in; a soft edge fades its top and foot, deeper at the foot while more lies below (`data-more`). The focused block never takes its chosen option's place: it opens at its top even when that option lies below the fold. On a short column (a phone turned sideways, a short window) the lines farthest from the focused question are dropped, one by one, until it has at least its header and first option, and a move puts the column back at its start (a question whose line was dropped is reset after its block is drawn again, so it opens at its top too); the first option is kept clear of the foot's 40 px soft edge; a tap on a line puts the keyboard focus on the chosen option only while it is in view, else on the first option, so Space never changes what the boss cannot see; a Ctrl+wheel (pinch or browser zoom) is the browser's and pages nothing. A folded line is one line (white space collapsed), has `aria-expanded="false"`, and is named by its place, title and answer.
- **A short window gives the questions the room.** At 560 px tall or less a card with several questions drops the unit's role · workspace line and "what it said", and sets its type and gaps smaller; a card with one question or a select keeps them until 340 px tall, where they no longer fit (measured: at 844×390 they fit, at 568×320 they overflow the card by 8 px). The open question's rows keep their size, so the own-words box is not squashed.
- **Tab walks the boxes.** In Zen and in Standard Tab goes from a question's option to its own-words box to its note and from the note to the next question's option; Shift+Tab from an option goes back to the last box of the question before (`askkeys.ts`, ZMV-F007). A key that moves the focus into another question first sends `ask-reveal` to the panel, because a browser cannot focus what is not drawn.
- **The send row is pinned.** Send, Cancel, the outcome line, and **Maybe later** stay in view at any window size, for one question as for several: on a dialog card the card's inner box is a column no taller than the page, the questions' area (the column, a lone question, or a select's options) gives way and scrolls inside the room that is left, and the send row does not shrink.
- **Size.** The column has a fixed height (`clamp(16rem, 58vh, 34rem)`, less on a short window), so the card's box does not follow the focus or the number of questions; the open block and its lines are centred in it, with a soft edge at both ends.
- A single-question dialog is left as it is (and still gets the room rule and the soft edge). The panel is wrapped once per dialog and is the same panel across snapshots, so a pick, a typed answer, and the focus survive them. Reduced motion has no fade.

### When an item leaves

An item leaves the screen in one of five ways; each is one transition of the model, with one sound at most:

| How | Sound | Then |
|---|---|---|
| The boss's answer or cancel is accepted | the knock, at the accepted outcome | the outcome line shows for 1.2 s (`accepted`), then the pause |
| **Not now** | none | the item rests; the pause |
| The item stops waiting by itself (answered in the terminal or on another device, the dialog closed, the unit moved on, a trust question settled, the unit's next question) | the knock, with the line "Answered elsewhere" | **the card stays** until the boss closes it (Enter, Esc, or its button *Back to the breath*), then the pause (Z-D20) |
| The unit leaves the net, or its pane closes | none | the card stays with 「這個單位已離開」 / *This unit has left*, then the pause when the boss closes it |
| The unit fails while on the screen (mark `error`) | the low bowl, with its error line | the same |

- An answer that is refused, stale, busy, or not confirmed (`said` gives the error tone) plays the low bowl and leaves the card on the screen with its outcome, as Standard's dialog does; a stale answer's item then leaves by itself on the next snapshot, and that exit plays nothing (one sound per thing the boss did).
- A new dialog on the same unit while it is current (another digest) is **not** swapped under the boss (Z-D20): the card he is reading stays, settled, and the new dialog queues for Enter. (Before Z-D20 `mountAsk` started afresh for it.)

### Skip and rest

- **Not now** rests the item for 10 minutes (O2). While it rests it is not eligible; when the rest ends it is eligible again in its place by age.
- A rest ends early when the item's question changes: a new dialog (another `dialog` and `digest`), or a unit that stopped waiting and waits again.
- Rests are this page's, in memory: a reload forgets them. Standard is not affected: its queue still holds the item.

### The pause

- After an item leaves, the card fades out and the breathing circle takes the middle of the screen for 3 s: one slow inhale over the 3 s (scale from 0.72 to 1). Then the companion fades in, silently; the next eligible item (a blocking question before any other, then the one that has waited longest: **Order**) waits for Enter (Z-D19, Z-D20). A question that arrives during the pause waits for it.
- A tap or click anywhere, Enter, or Space ends the pause at once. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the circle stands still and the pause is still 3 s.
- While the page is hidden, a pause that would end ends at once when the page is shown again (no one was there to breathe). A hidden page that has an item waiting does not keep the pause at all: the pause ends, the item waits for Enter, and the page reminds (below). With nothing waiting, the pause is kept until the page is shown. 0.59.4 changed this: in the 2026-11-05 trial a real question from a box stayed off the page after an earlier card's pause, because the page was hidden, and the pause was kept for a boss who was not there to breathe.

### The visual language

Zen's components are Standard's in ink; Z-D15 takes the boxes off them. The block sits at the end of `gui/zen.css` under its own comment, and `tests/gui-css.test.ts` pins it.

1. **One focus.** At any moment one thing is in `--zen-paper` at full weight: what to read or answer now. The rest is ash, fainter, or hidden.
2. **No boxes.** Choices, the log, the menu, the music list and the palette have no frame or panel; space, line height and the weight of the type part them. The one line is the hairline under a text field. Borders turn `transparent`, never `0` or `none`, so forced colours still draws them.
3. **Selection is light.** A hovered or keyed choice brightens and takes a faint warm ground (`--zen-glow`); no pill grows. A choice carries a small dot before it: hollow, filled in `--zen-paper` when chosen.
4. **The seal marks only the keys.** `--zen-seal` shows where the keyboard is: the dot's ring for a choice, a line under a word for a button, the field's hairline for a text field. Never a ring around a box, and never body text. The `#zen` rules sit above the page-wide focus ring at the top of the sheet.
5. **Overlays let the page recede.** The palette, the menu and the music list lay `--zen-veil` with a blur over the page and float on the ink, with no panel. The veil takes the press that closes them, so it never reaches the card under it (ZVL-F002); in the terminal layer the confirm pair is two words as well (ZVL-F001), and under forced colours the chosen dot is `Highlight` (ZVL-F003).
6. **Type, not components, makes the layers.** Questions and messages in the serif; names, times and hints in the smaller ash sans; code in monospace on a faint `--zen-tint`. What you said sits at the right in ash, with no ground; the other side at the left in paper.

The new colours are tokens beside the ink (`--zen-glow`, `--zen-tint`, `--zen-veil`, `--zen-seal-glow`), so the block names no colour of its own. Standard and Geek are unchanged.

### Motion

- **Breathing circle**: a ring and a faint radial fill, `min(48vmin, 320px)` across, one CSS animation of `transform` and `opacity` only: 10 s per breath, 4 s in (scale 0.72 to 1, ease-in-out) and 6 s out. It runs while nothing is current (the companion, or none) and drives the pause.
- **Drift**: two full-page layers (`--zen-ground` and `--zen-ground-2` in a soft radial gradient) cross-fade by `opacity` over 90 s, back and forth. Only compositor properties move, so the page does not repaint per frame.
- Cards fade in and out (opacity, 400 ms); nothing slides or bounces.
- **The card's size**: a card takes the size its content needs at once; nothing animates a card's height or width (Z-D9, **Polish round**; the 520 ms easing of Z-D7 is gone, with `zensize.ts`). A swap of what the card holds fades instead: the card's parts arrive by opacity alone (`zen-in`, 1.8 s, one after another), and a new dialog on the same unit, the words that stand for a dialog that went, and an outcome line that appears set `data-fade` on their element (`fade` in `gui/zen.ts`), which runs `zen-fade` for 0.3 s (opacity only). The first drawing of a card is its entrance, not a swap. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing fades. A card swapped for another dissolves while the next one begins, as a card that leaves does. The stage is one row as tall as the page, so a card taller than the page is clamped to it and scrolls inside.
- **Ripples** (`gui/zenripple.ts`): where a mouse or pen passes over the ground it leaves a soft ripple, three faint concentric rings that spread and fade over 1.6 s, on one canvas behind all content (`pointer-events: none`). At most 12 a second (85 ms apart, 28 px of travel apart), at most 20 alive, drawn by `requestAnimationFrame` only while one lives. A touch's move (a scroll) leaves none, a tap one. Off under `prefers-reduced-motion`, under forced colours (the canvas is hidden), while the page is hidden (what lives is dropped) and while it is calm (30 s without input: no new ones, so the loop ends). The rings are 1.25 px strokes of `rgb(120, 106, 88)` at alpha 0.06 at the brightest; a pixel is budgeted four overlapping rings, and the ground with four peak rings still holds every text colour at 7 : 1 (`tests/zen-ripple.test.ts`).
- **Calm rules**: Zen is not Geek, so `calm.ts` runs its timer in Zen: after 30 s without input `data-calm` stills the circle (at its exhaled size) and the drift (on `--zen-ground`), and any input brings them back. **(Superseded by Z-D13, 2026-11-03: `data-calm` stills nothing in Zen; the circle and the ground keep breathing, and only the ripples rest by it.)** Under `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing moves at all, from the first frame (the stylesheet's media query; `calm.ts` already does nothing there). Under `forced-colors` the system colours take over: the drift layers are hidden, the circle is a `CanvasText` outline, and the card a plain border.
- **Phone and desktop**: one layout that scales: the card centred with the corner controls in the safe-area insets; on a phone the card takes the width, long questions scroll inside it with the existing more-below cue (`cue`, `gui/ask.ts`); every control is at least 44 by 44 px; no sideways scroll at 320 px.

## Sound

### Effects

Synthesized in `gui/zensound.ts` with Web Audio, no files:

| Effect | When | Recipe (starting point, tuned in the live check) |
|---|---|---|
| **Bowl** | an item takes the screen | partials at 228 Hz × 1, 2.71, 5.15, 8.42 with gains 1, 0.45, 0.2, 0.08; each partial two sine oscillators 1.2 Hz apart (the bowl's beating); 15 ms attack, exponential decay with time constants 2.4, 1.4, 0.8, 0.4 s; 5 s long; peak 0.35 of the effects bus |
| **Knock** (木魚) | an item is done (**When an item leaves**) | a sine from 560 Hz falling to 480 Hz over 40 ms plus a 25 ms noise burst through a band-pass at 1.1 kHz (Q 6); decay time constant 70 ms; 250 ms long; peak 0.5 |
| **Low bowl** | an error of an item on the screen or of the boss's answer | the bowl's partials on 146 Hz, decay time constants two thirds of the bowl's, a 1 % downward bend over its length; peak 0.3 |

- Each recipe is a plain object (partials, envelopes, filter) that a pure function turns into scheduled nodes on any `BaseAudioContext`, so tests check the schedule with a fake context.
- **On and off**: the effects follow the settings page's Sound switch (`sb.sound`, off by default), the one switch that lets the page make sounds (O3). Zen never plays `notify.ts`'s clips, and Standard and Geek never play Zen's effects.
- **Volume**: the settings' Volume (`sb.volume`), as the square of it, read at each play, so a change on the settings page applies at once.
- While the music plays, an effect ducks it by 6 dB over 0.3 s and lets it back over 1.5 s.

### Music

**Z6 as built (2026-11-03) differs from the first design on where the tracks come from.** No tracks are bundled (O7 and O8 stay unresolved, and the package does not grow): the boss drops audio files into `straw-boss-music/` in the agent folder, beside the sb state directory and not inside it, so the Windows owner-only ACL step never walks it and a link there works (`$PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR/straw-boss-music/`, by default `~/.pi/agent/straw-boss-music/`; the same folder for every Herdr slot), and the GUI's server serves them.

- **The folder and its route** (`src/music.ts`, routed in `src/gui.ts`, the folder handed over by `src/server/guihost.ts`). `GET /api/music` lists the folder's audio files as `{ tracks: [{ file, size }] }` (sorted by name with numbers by value, at most 200); `GET /api/music/<file>` streams one, with `Range` requests (206 with `Content-Range`; 416 `bytes */size` for a start past the end; one range only, anything else is answered whole) because an `<audio>` element seeks and an iPhone asks for ranges only. The folder is read at each ask, so a file dropped in shows the next time Zen shows. It sits behind the token, the Host check, and the paired-device cookie like every other `/api` read (a paired device reads it over the tailnet and over Wi-Fi), and only GET reads it. A track is a file that the folder's own listing holds by its exact name, of type mp3, m4a, aac, ogg, oga, opus, wav, or flac (the content type follows the extension, sent with `nosniff`), not hidden (a leading dot, AppleDouble `._x`), not empty, regular, and whose real path stays inside the folder's real path. Sub folders, a name with a slash, a backslash, or a control character, `..`, another letter case of a real name, a link that leaves the folder, and a dangling link are 404. With no folder, an empty one, or one that is not there, the list is empty and **♪** is not shown.
- `gui/zenmusic.ts` is a chunk of its own, loaded once the music is turned on (or is stored on and Zen shows, so the first gesture can play at once). The controls (`gui/zenmusicbar.ts`) are in the Zen chunk and read the list (a few hundred bytes) each time Zen shows. No audio byte is fetched before a track plays.
- **Playback**: two `<audio preload="none">` elements, each through `createMediaElementSource` and a gain of its own into the music bus (an iPhone ignores an element's `volume`, and honours a gain). The music bus (`gui/audiobus.ts`, half the effects' level) reaches the master through Zen's duck node (`gui/zensound.ts`), so an effect ducks the music by 6 dB, under the one Volume. The chosen track plays on one element; 6 s before its end (half the length for a track under 12 s) the other starts the same track from 0 and the two cross-fade (equal-power curves over 6 s), so the loop has no gap (an MP3's encoder padding makes `loop = true` click). An end reached with no fade begun plays the track again. Nothing is decoded into an `AudioBuffer`. A play the browser refuses (`NotAllowedError`), a file that fails (the element's `error`, any other rejection), and a context it will not resume leave the switch on and say **tap to play**; a play interrupted by a pause or a new load says nothing.
- **Level**: the music bus is the effects' level × 0.5, under the same Volume.
- **Controls**: the corner's **♪** toggles the music with one tap (`aria-pressed`, the state in a word in its label: "Music: on", "Music: off", or "Music: tap to play"); the **⌄** beside it (shown when the folder holds two tracks or more) opens a popover like the corner menu's (a press opens it with the focus on the playing track; the arrows and Tab move through the tracks; Esc, a press outside, and a pick close it, and Zen's Esc table counts it as an open menu) with the tracks as radio buttons, named by the file name without its extension. **Try** for the effects is the corner menu's (Z5), not this list's. `localStorage` keeps `sb.zen.music` (`"1"`/`"0"`, default `"0"`) and `sb.zen.track` (a file name; a missing or unknown one is the first track). A track picked while the music plays takes over with the same 6 s cross-fade; picked while it is off, it is only stored.
- **Hidden page**: on a touch screen (`pointer: coarse`) the music pauses when the page is hidden and resumes when it is shown (a resume the browser refuses says tap to play); on a desktop it keeps playing (O6). A paused element keeps its place.
- The music plays in every Zen screen, an item's included, and stops when the page leaves Zen (away). On coming back it starts again at once if the page has played before (the context is unlocked), else it waits for a gesture as at load.

### Autoplay and unlocking on phones

- Browsers start an `AudioContext` suspended until a user gesture (iOS Safari, Chrome on Android, desktop Chrome under its autoplay policy). Zen creates its context at the first `pointerdown` or `keydown` on the page (capture, once; the music takes the first `pointerup` or `keydown`, because an iPhone counts a press only when it ends) and resumes it there; before that, an effect that is due is dropped, never queued (a bowl that rings late is about nothing).
- The music starts only from a gesture: a tap on **♪**, or, when `sb.zen.music` is on from before, the first gesture anywhere on the page outside the music controls (which act for themselves). Until then **♪** shows "Music: tap to play".
- iOS suspends the context when the page is hidden or the phone locks, and may refuse `resume()` without a gesture afterwards; when `resume()` fails or the context stays `suspended` or `interrupted`, Zen waits for the next gesture and **♪** says "tap to play" again.
- iOS mutes Web Audio under the ringer switch; Zen does not set `navigator.audioSession.type`, so the switch keeps working as the boss expects. The live check confirms both on the boss's iPhone.

### Music tracks (superseded by the music folder)

The first design had a separate worker produce three Suno tracks into `gui/sounds/zen/` (instrumental, MP3, at most 7.5 MB in all, with `tracks.ts` and a `CREDITS.md` that recorded the licence). Z6 as built ships none (**Music**, above); a track the boss puts in the music folder is the boss's own file and not part of the package. If tracks are bundled later, O8 must be answered first.

## Reminders, the tab icon, and the other views' sounds

- In Zen, `notify.signal` is not called for a snapshot (no Standard clip, no notification per arrival). Instead, when an item takes the screen while the page is hidden or unfocused, Zen calls a new `notify.remind(alert)`: the same system notification Standard would show for that alert, behind the same Remind switch and permission. A hidden page whose pause ends because an item waits (above) counts as the item taking the screen: the reminder follows. (Z-D20: no card takes the screen by itself, so the rule is one reminder for each card that waits, a card behind the one on the screen included, and only while the page is hidden or unfocused; a page that is looked at gets none, and a card is reminded once while the server lists it.) Clicking the notification brings the page forward on that item.
- The tab icon carries no count while Zen is shown (the plain icon); away, Standard's badge comes back with the next snapshot.
- Standard's and Geek's `notify.ts` clips, switches, and notifications are unchanged.

## Wiring in `main.ts`

The changes to the page's coordinator, kept small and named here so the step's diff is reviewable:

- `zenOn()`: the resolved view is `zen`, the page is not away, and the route is not `settings`. `view.onChange` and `navigate` load the Zen chunk the first time `zenOn()` holds, and show or hide `#zen`.
- `show(net)`: while `zenOn()`, after `linkBar.net`, it hands the alerts map (`alertsOf`) and the net to Zen instead of `notify.signal`, skips the badge, and returns as the hidden-page path does, so Standard draws the newest snapshot on leaving (`show(pending)`, `page.layout` on the next frame).
- The stream's watch is `watchNow()`: Zen's companion key while Zen is shown, else `watchOf(current)`; `forward` hands history events to Zen too.
- A capture-phase Escape listener gives the key to the layer first while Zen shows (**Leaving and coming back**), and the page's own Escape goes on as it does today when Zen does not show; the hotkeys and the drawer check `zenOn()`. View moves go through `mountFace` (`gui/viewface.ts`): `face.leave()` for Esc and the exits, `face.route()` for the settings route, and `view.onChange` for picks and follows.

## Risks

- **A view that hides things on purpose.** With no counts and no notices, the boss can miss a failure or a long queue while he sits in Zen. Standard is one Esc away and keeps everything; the tab icon's count comes back there. Whether failures should become items is O1.
- **iOS audio.** Media elements through `createMediaElementSource`, resuming after a lock, and the ringer switch have changed between iOS releases; the live check covers the boss's phone, and a failure leaves Zen silent, never stuck (**Autoplay**).
- **Track licence and size.** Shipping Suno tracks in an MIT npm package depends on the account's plan (O8); the package grows by several megabytes (O7). **(As built in Z6: the tracks are the boss's own files in a server-served music folder; see Music.)**
- **Two views of one queue.** An item skipped in Zen is still first in Standard's queue, and an item answered in Standard leaves Zen; both read the same snapshots, so they never contradict each other about what waits, only about what is shown first.
- **The composer's wait.** An item that waits while the boss is at a draft in the companion's composer waits at most 20 s after his last touch of the companion, whether or not the box has the focus; typing keeps renewing it, so someone typing is never interrupted and someone who walks away from a focused box is (O10).

### Z4d: the item's context

The boss's decision 「可展開的完整歷史」: an item's card carries a **前情（H）** link, shown when the unit has a session log (`hasHistory` in `gui/zenhistory.ts`: every pi pane, an outside one too; a tower only while it runs one; never a trust question). A tap or **H** expands the unit's session log inside the card, read-only; the same again, or Esc (first in the Esc order above), collapses it.

- It is lazy: the log's chunk is fetched, mounted, and watched only on the first expand, and a collapse, a card that leaves, or a hide of Zen drops all three.
- Answering stays live while it is open: the history is what the boss reads to decide, so the dialog and Not now keep working.
- The link has `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` for the region; closing from inside the region returns the focus to the link.
- Who gets the link: the units listed above; a card without one answers H with nothing.
